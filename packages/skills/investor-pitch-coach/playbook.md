# Investor Pitch Coach — Agent Skill

Comprehensive guidance for helping founders prepare investor-grade pitches and assess investment readiness. Based on synthesized frameworks from Guy Kawasaki, Y Combinator, Sequoia, and the Athar Investment methodology.

---

## 1. The 8 Investment Readiness Signals

Investors (seed-stage angels, accelerators, VCs) look for these 8 things, in roughly this order of weight:

### Signal 1: Strong Team (Weight: ~30%)
What they assess:
- Domain expertise: does the team understand the problem deeply from lived experience?
- Diversity of skills: does the team cover business, technology, and product?
- Deep market understanding: can they name specific customers and their exact pains?
- Self-confidence: do they believe in the thesis without being delusional?
- Speed: evidence of shipping fast and learning from failure?
- Commitment: full-time? Skin in the game?

**Agent Rule:** If a founder is presenting solo with no domain expertise in the problem space, flag this as a critical risk. Investors often pass on great ideas with weak teams over weak ideas with great teams.

### Signal 2: Promising Market (Weight: ~25%)
What they assess:
- Large AND growing market (not just large — a declining large market is a trap)
- High profit potential (now or in a credible future)
- Weak competition OR a specific competitive advantage that can win

**How to present market size correctly:**
DO: 'The Egyptian SME accounting software market is $X million (Source: Y). Our SAM based on our ICP is X companies (Apollo data). Our realistic Year 1 SOM is X customers based on our outreach capacity.'
DON'T: 'The global accounting software market is $15 billion' (irrelevant for an early-stage Egyptian SME play)

### Signal 3: Strong Product (Weight: ~20%)
What they assess:
- Smooth, intuitive user experience (frictionless demo is mandatory)
- Technology built in-house by the founding team (outsourced tech is a red flag at seed)
- Evidence of product management discipline: they understand what to build and what not to build

### Signal 4: Scalable Business Model (Weight: ~10%)
What they assess:
- Revenue grows faster than costs as the company scales
- Unit economics are either healthy or have a credible path to health
- There is a clear answer to 'how does this get to $10M ARR?'

Scalability factors to assess (rate 1-10):
- Single core product (focus vs. scattered features)
- Light asset base (software over physical assets)
- Product ecosystem (do partners do work on your platform?)
- Automated processes (what % of operations is automated?)
- Replicability (can the model copy-paste to new geographies?)

### Signal 5: Promising Brand (Weight: ~5%)
What they assess:
- Memorable, distinctive name (not generic)
- Clear marketing capability (can they tell the story simply?)
- Evidence of exceptional customer experience (NPS, testimonials, case studies)

### Signal 6: Validated Traction (Weight: ~5%)
This is often the most decisive signal at seed stage.
- Investors back products, not ideas — revenue, active users, or signed LOIs matter more than features
- Even 3 paying customers is stronger than a 50-page business plan
- Show month-over-month growth, not one-time spikes

### Signal 7: Clear Plan (Weight: ~3%)
What they assess:
- Specific expansion plan (not 'we will expand to MENA')
- Detailed marketing plan for the next 12 months
- Financial plan: what happens to their money, burn rate, runway, break-even
- Product roadmap: what will be built with the funding?
- Specific funding ask: how much, for what, with what milestones?
- Defensibility: how do you protect against big competitors copying you?

### Signal 8: Persuasion Ability (Weight: ~2%)
- Can the founder communicate the vision clearly in 60 seconds?
- Do they handle hard questions without deflecting?
- Do they inspire confidence that they can recruit, sell, and lead?

## 2. Funding Stage to Startup Stage Mapping

| Funding Stage | Startup Stage Required | Typical Check Size | What You Need |
|--------------|----------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Pre-Seed / Incubator | Stage 1-2 (Empathy + early Stickiness) | $0-$100K | Problem validation + MVP |
| Seed / Angel | Stage 2-3 (Stickiness + early Virality) | $100K-$500K | Product + early traction |
| Series A | Stage 4 (Revenue + unit economics) | $1M-$5M | Repeatable revenue growth |
| Series B+ | Stage 5 (Scaling) | $5M+ | Proven growth engine |

**Agent Rule:** When a founder asks 'am I ready to raise?', map their current state to this table first. If they are at Stage 1 seeking Series A money, the honest answer is 'Not yet — here is what you need to get to before fundraising.'

## 3. The 10-Slide Pitch Deck (Detailed Breakdown)

### Slide 1: Title
Content: Company name, one-liner, founder name, contact, date
One-liner formula: 'We help [specific ICP] [achieve outcome] without [pain/sacrifice]'
Time: 30 seconds

### Slide 2: Problem
Content: The specific problem your ICP faces daily. Make it vivid and tangible.
Structure:
- Who: the specific customer (not 'businesses' — 'Egyptian restaurant owners with 5+ branches')
- What: the exact pain (costs them X EGP/month in wasted ops, or causes X frustrated customers per week)
- Why now: why is this the right moment for this solution?
Common mistake: Generic problem statements ('people need better software'). Be hyper-specific.
Evidence required: At minimum, reference real customer quotes or interview statistics.

### Slide 3: Solution
Content: The 'aha' moment. One clear sentence. Then the demo or screenshot.
Rules:
- Lead with the outcome, not the feature ('Restaurants reduce ops errors by 80%' not 'An AI-powered operations platform')
- Show, don't describe — live demo or product screenshots are mandatory
- One primary value proposition; save the feature list for the appendix

### Slide 4: Business Model
Content: How you make money. Be specific.
Structure:
- Revenue model: subscription / transaction fee / usage-based / freemium-to-paid
- Price point: what do customers pay? Per seat? Per month? Per transaction?
- Unit economics: what is your target CAC and LTV?
Common mistake: Vague statements like 'we will monetize through multiple revenue streams'. Pick one primary model and defend it.

### Slide 5: Underlying Magic / Why We Win
Content: What gives you an unfair advantage?
Options:
- Proprietary technology
- Unique data or network effects
- Regulatory or partnership advantage
- Domain expertise the team has that competitors lack
- Speed: 'We shipped in 3 weeks what took competitors 6 months'
Common mistake: Listing generic features as advantages ('better UX', 'more features'). These are not moats.

### Slide 6: Go-to-Market
Content: How will you acquire your first 100 customers? Your first 1000?
Structure:
- Channel: where do your customers discover new solutions? (LinkedIn, referrals, cold outreach, community?)
- Beachhead: what is the smallest, most winnable initial market segment?
- CAC hypothesis: what do you expect to pay to acquire each customer through this channel?
- Viral or referral mechanics: does the product spread naturally?

### Slide 7: Competitive Landscape
Content: Honest positioning vs. all alternatives.
Rules:
- ALWAYS include 'do nothing / status quo' as a competitor
- ALWAYS include indirect competitors (Excel, WhatsApp, manual processes)
- 2x2 matrix: Price (low-high) vs. Value (low-high). Where do you sit? Where do competitors sit?
Common mistake: Claiming 'there is no competition'. This either means the market doesn't exist, or the founder hasn't looked hard enough.

### Slide 8: Team
Content: Why this team, for this problem, at this moment?
Structure:
- Relevant domain experience (years doing the exact job you are automating)
- Technical capability (who builds the product?)
- Business/sales capability (who sells and grows?)
- Prior startup experience or notable achievements
- Advisors or investors who add credibility
Common mistake: Generic LinkedIn summaries. Focus on what makes this team uniquely qualified for THIS specific problem.

### Slide 9: Financial Projections
Content: 18-month detailed plan; 3-year high-level projection.
Must include:
- Revenue projections with stated assumptions (number of customers x ARPU)
- Cost breakdown: salaries, infrastructure, marketing, operations
- Burn rate per month
- Runway with current/raised funding
- Break-even point (month/year)
- Key financial milestones: 'We reach break-even at 120 customers in month 14'
Common mistake: Over-optimistic hockey-stick curves with no stated assumptions. State every assumption explicitly.

### Slide 10: Current Status and Ask
Content: Where you are today + what you need.
Structure:
- Traction: customers, revenue, users, partnerships, letters of intent
- Key milestones achieved: product shipped, first paying customer, pilot launched
- What you are raising: specific amount (e.g. $200K seed)
- Use of funds: specific allocation (60% salaries, 25% marketing, 15% infrastructure)
- What this funding achieves: 'By month 12, we will have X customers and $Y ARR'
- Timeline: when do you need to close?

## 4. Financial Planning Reference

### 18-Month Financial Model Template
Every startup should be able to answer:
- Month 1-6: Revenue per month, customers acquired, CAC, burn rate
- Month 7-12: Scaling assumptions, team additions, marketing spend
- Month 13-18: Path to break-even or next raise

### Key Metrics for AI/Tech Startups
| Metric | What It Measures | Target |
|--------|-----------------|--------|
| Inference cost per API call | Core COGS for AI products | Trending down with scale |
| Gross Margin | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | >70% for software, >50% for AI with compute costs |
| CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) | Marketing spend / New customers | Depends on LTV; CAC < LTV/3 |
| LTV (Lifetime Value) | Avg revenue per customer x avg lifetime | LTV > 3x CAC |
| Payback Period | CAC / Monthly revenue per customer | <12 months for SaaS |
| Burn Rate | Monthly cash outflow | Runway > 18 months |
| Runway | Cash / Burn rate | At least 12-18 months always |
| MRR / ARR | Monthly/Annual recurring revenue | Month-over-month growth rate |
| Churn Rate | % customers lost per month | <5% monthly for SaaS |
| NPS (Net Promoter Score) | Customer satisfaction proxy | >50 is excellent |

### Revenue Model Archetypes
| Model | How It Works | Best For |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| Per-seat subscription | Monthly fee per user | B2B SaaS |
| Usage-based | Pay per API call / document / query | AI tools, API products |
| Flat subscription | Single monthly fee | Simple tools |
| Transaction fee | % of each transaction | Marketplaces, fintech |
| Freemium to paid | Free basic, paid premium | Consumer, developer tools |

## 5. Business Plan Structure (for Structured Pitches)

When a founder needs a written business plan (for accelerators, grants, or formal investors):

**Section 1: Executive Summary**
One page maximum. Problem, solution, market, team, ask.

**Section 2: Company Overview**
Mission, vision, values, legal structure, stage.

**Section 3: Problem and Solution**
Detailed problem analysis + value proposition + differentiation.

**Section 4: Market Analysis**
TAM/SAM/SOM with sources. Customer segments. Competitive landscape.

**Section 5: Business Model**
Revenue streams, pricing, unit economics, cost structure.

**Section 6: Go-to-Market Strategy**
Customer acquisition channels, partnerships, marketing plan.

**Section 7: Product/Service Description**
Current state, roadmap, underlying technology.

**Section 8: Team**
Founders, key hires, advisors, board.

**Section 9: Financial Plan**
18-month detailed + 3-year projections. Funding history and current raise.

**Section 10: Risk Analysis (SWOT)**
Honest assessment of risks with mitigation strategies.

## 6. Demo Day Pitch Coaching (Hackathon Context)

For the AI-OS Validation Copilot demo specifically:

**The 7-Beat Demo Script (from demo-script.md):**
1. Open with the founder's real problem — the firsthand validation failure story (the WHY)
2. Run the golden path LIVE on a real idea
3. Show the trace panel in real time — sources, tool calls, verifier checks (proof against hallucination)
4. Trigger the planted-error case on purpose — show the Verifier catching an unsupported claim
5. Show a second idea from a different domain — demonstrates methodology generalizes
6. Show the metrics table from golden-task evaluation
7. Close with the HERMAS platform roadmap (Skill Packs 2 and 3)

**Key Messages for Judges:**
- This does what most founders skip: rigorous secondary research + real primary evidence collection
- It refuses to call something 'validated' without real user evidence (show the code enforcement)
- The Verifier layer makes hallucination bounded, measured, and disclosed — not eliminated, but controlled
- Domain-agnostic: methodology is the constant; domain knowledge is fetched at runtime

**Fail-Safe:** Have a screen recording of a full successful run saved. If live demo fails, say so plainly and show the recording. Do NOT present a recording as live.
